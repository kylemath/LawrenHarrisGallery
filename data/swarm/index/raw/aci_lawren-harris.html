Art for Truth and Reconciliation

Welcome to the Art Canada Institute, your definitive online source for Canadian art. Through our expertly authored books, engaging educational programs, and pay-wall free digital resources, we bring the remarkable stories of Canada’s artists to audiences everywhere.

What We Do

We are the go-to platform for exploring Canada’s art through our online library, newsletter, exhibitions, and resources for teachers. We work with Canada’s leading art historians, curators, and visual culture experts to share their knowledge and expertise so that you can connect with the art and artists of Canada. Discover the people, places, stories, and ideas that shape this country’s art.


Why We Do It

Canada’s art is an essential part of our shared identity. We believe everyone should be able to connect with it and understand it, regardless of their prior knowledge or access to a bricks-and-mortar museum. When we founded the Art Canada Institute as a not-for-profit educational organization in 2013, there was no national online resource dedicated to sharing information about this country’s art and artists. We saw a need and filled the gap.

Art, Truth & Reconciliation

By Gerald McMaster


Between 2008 and 2015, the Truth and Reconciliation Commission of Canada documented the lasting impact of Canada’s Indian Residential Schools on Indigenous Peoples and created 94 Calls to Action. Although the report profoundly reshaped the nation’s cultural consciousness, until now, no comprehensive surveys have been conducted to see how Indigenous artists have responded to this chapter in Canadian history.

2025 Canadian Art Inspiration Student Challenge

How do you raise awareness? What do your family traditions look like? What is your most prized possession? In celebration of ACI’s fifth annual Student Challenge, we asked questions such as these to students across Canada in the hopes of finding remarkable talent influenced by our country’s most innovative makers. Regina Art
& Artists:
An Illustrated History

With a population of less than 300,000 residents, Regina has produced a disproportionately large number of artists and artistic currents relative to its size—including three modern art movements of national significance. The birthplace of the Regina Five and Regina Clay, and home to some of the most important Indigenous artists working in Canada today, Saskatchewan’s capital has long been a vital site for creative innovation.
